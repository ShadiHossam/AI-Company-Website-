# AI Security Review in Abu Dhabi: The Checklist IT Will Run Before You Automate Customer Chat

You picked a vendor. The demo went well. Then your IT lead asks where the conversation data sits, which law applies, and what happens when a customer types a card number into WhatsApp.

An AI security review in Abu Dhabi rarely fails on the technology. It fails because nobody prepared answers to those three questions before the contract landed on someone's desk.

This is the checklist your internal security function will run, in the order they will run it.

## Key Takeaways

- Mainland, DIFC and ADGM businesses sit under three separate data protection laws, not one "UAE data law". Confirm which applies before the review starts.
- Abu Dhabi IT teams ask about incident response frameworks such as NIST SP 800-61, SIEM logging and analyst certifications. Have written answers ready.
- WhatsApp Business API needs a valid trade licence and a display-name review before go-live, and Meta's per-category billing has been revised more than once.
- Set explicit red lines: no card data storage, no unsupervised refunds, no indefinite transcript retention, no training on your customer data by default.
- A one-off review and a full-time in-house hire sit in different cost brackets. Decide what the business actually needs before comparing quotes.

## Which UAE Data Law Actually Applies to Your AI Chat Setup

There is no single "UAE data law". Three separate regimes exist, and which one governs your AI chat tool depends entirely on where your entity is registered. Confirm that first, because it decides everything else in the review.

Mainland-licensed companies, the ones holding an ADDED licence and registered through TAMM, fall under the federal Personal Data Protection Law, Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021. That is the default for most Abu Dhabi clinics, dealerships and service firms.

ADGM-registered entities follow the ADGM Data Protection Regulations 2021 instead. Different regulator, different text, different obligations.

DIFC-registered companies sit under DIFC Data Protection Law No. 5 of 2020. This one matters more than people expect for chat automation, because it explicitly addresses automated decision-making.

If your AI assistant triages enquiries, scores leads or auto-responds without a person reading the message first, that is automated processing and DIFC law has something to say about it.

Get this wrong and the rest of the review is theatre. A vendor answering federal PDPL questions for a DIFC entity has answered the wrong exam paper.

## The Vendor Security Questions an Abu Dhabi IT Team Will Raise First

Expect four lines of questioning: incident response, logging, who reviews the setup, and what the tool will refuse to do. These map directly to what Abu Dhabi security teams are hired to do.

**Incident response process.** Ask whether the vendor's process follows a documented framework. Abu Dhabi SOC job specifications list incident response coordination aligned to NIST SP 800-61, so that is the vocabulary your internal team already uses. A vendor who answers "we'd email you" has told you enough.

**Logging and monitoring.** Which SIEM receives chatbot activity? Local SOC hiring requirements name Splunk, Sentinel and QRadar for alert triage, which means your security function is likely already running one of them. The useful question is whether chat events can be exported into it, not whether the vendor has a pretty dashboard.

**Who signs off.** Abu Dhabi employers advertising for this exact work list GIAC Certified Intrusion Analyst (GCIA), Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH) and Security+ CE as the credentials they want. Use the same bar for whoever reviews your deployment, whether that is staff or an outside consultant.

**The written "will not" list.** This is the one most businesses skip. Ask for a document stating what the tool will not do with conversation data: no training of shared models on your customer chats by default, no data leaving the agreed storage location, no silent changes to either.

If it is not written, it is not a commitment. UAE authorities have publicly [warned users to check permissions before granting camera or location access](https://gulfnews.com/uae/uae-warns-ai-users-to-check-permissions-before-granting-camera-or-location-access-1.500656300) to AI applications. The same scrutiny applies to a business deployment.

## WhatsApp Business API and Malaffi: Two Abu Dhabi Wrinkles

Generic AI security advice misses two things in this market entirely. One is how WhatsApp onboarding actually works. The other is what happens when a clinic connects patient data to an assistant.

WhatsApp Business API onboarding requires a valid trade licence and a display-name review before the number can go live for automated chat. That is a gate, not a formality. If your licence details and your intended display name do not line up, the number does not go live and your launch date moves.

Budgeting is the second trap. Meta bills conversations separately by category, marketing, utility, authentication and service, and has revised that pricing more than once. Treat it as a category review rather than a fixed monthly rate, and have someone map which category your actual message volume falls into before you sign anything.

For clinics, Malaffi changes the shape of the review completely. The Abu Dhabi health information exchange means patient data moves between providers, so scope precisely what the AI assistant is permitted to read or write inside it before automation starts.

"The bot books appointments" is not a scope. "The bot reads appointment slots and writes nothing back" is.

Procurement adds a third layer. ICV certification and TAMM-linked government contracts can make a documented AI security review a contractual condition rather than a nice-to-have. If you sell to government entities, the review document becomes part of your bid file.

## What the AI Chat Tool Should Never Be Allowed to Do

Four red lines. Write them into the contract, not the kickoff call.

**It should not store or forward payment card numbers.** Customers type card details into WhatsApp threads. They do it constantly.

The tool needs to detect that pattern, refuse to retain it and hand the customer a proper payment link instead. Ask the vendor to demonstrate this, not describe it.

**It should not action refunds, cancellations or booking changes without human sign-off.** Automation that reads and drafts is low risk. Automation that executes a financial or scheduling change on its own is a different category. Keep a person in that loop even when it slows things down.

**It should not retain full transcripts indefinitely.** Ask for a stated deletion point, in days, written down. "We keep them for analytics" is an answer that will not survive a regulator's question about storage limitation.

**It should not train a shared or public model on your enquiry data by default.** Default matters here. Plenty of tools allow you to opt out, and plenty of businesses never check the setting. Confirm the default position in writing, then confirm what your account is actually set to.

A useful test of vendor seriousness: ask what protections exist around the system instructions themselves. If a customer can talk the assistant into revealing its configuration or ignoring its rules, every red line above becomes optional. ## In-House Security Hire or Outside Review: What It Actually Costs

A one-off review and a permanent hire are not competing options at the same price point. They solve different problems, and the numbers make that obvious.

Abu Dhabi job postings for AI and automation engineering roles run from AED10,000 a month for an n8n AI engineer position up to AED33,000 a month for a GenAI specialist role, according to local listings. Annualised, that is a standing cost before you count recruitment, visas or the security tooling the person will ask for.

Outside review sits elsewhere. Clutch-listed AI consultancies serving Dubai quote hourly bands from $25-49 at the low end through to $150-199, with stated minimum project sizes ranging from $1,000+ up to $50,000+. AED is pegged at roughly 3.67 to the US dollar, so those figures convert directly for budgeting.

| Option | Indicative cost | Best suited to |

The deciding question is not price. It is whether you need a fixed-scope pre-launch review or continuous SOC-style monitoring after go-live. Those are different products, and quotes for one tell you nothing useful about the other.

Most businesses automating customer chat for the first time need the review, not the headcount. The monitoring question becomes real once volume and data sensitivity grow, and it is worth revisiting annually rather than deciding once.

## FAQ

**Does the UAE have one data protection law that covers AI chatbots?**
No. Mainland companies fall under the federal Personal Data Protection Law, Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021, DIFC entities under DIFC Data Protection Law No. 5 of 2020, and ADGM entities under the ADGM Data Protection Regulations 2021.

Your registration jurisdiction decides which one applies to your chat tool.

**Do I need a trade licence before connecting WhatsApp Business API for AI chat?**
Yes. WhatsApp Business API onboarding requires a valid trade licence plus a display-name review before the number goes live for automated chat. Sort both before you plan a launch date.

**Does DIFC law treat AI chatbot decisions differently from federal UAE law?**
DIFC Data Protection Law No. 5 of 2020 explicitly covers automated decision-making, which is directly relevant if your AI tool triages or auto-responds to enquiries. That makes automated triage a specific point of review for DIFC-registered entities rather than a general one.

**What certifications should I look for when vetting an AI security reviewer in Abu Dhabi?**
Abu Dhabi employers hiring for this work list GIAC Certified Intrusion Analyst (GCIA), Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH) and Security+ CE. Ask which of these the person actually reviewing your deployment holds, and whether they have worked with the SIEM platform your business runs.

**Is it cheaper to hire an in-house AI security engineer or pay for a one-off review?**
For a single pre-launch assessment, a scoped external review is the smaller spend. Abu Dhabi AI engineering roles are advertised from AED10,000 to AED33,000 a month, while consultancy minimums start from $1,000+ at the low end. The comparison changes if you need continuous monitoring rather than one review.

**Does Malaffi affect how a clinic can use AI chat for patient enquiries?**
Yes. If your clinic is connected to the Malaffi health information exchange, scope exactly what patient data the AI assistant may read or write inside it before automation begins. Define read and write permissions separately rather than granting blanket access.

**What should an AI chat tool never be allowed to do with customer conversations?**
It should not store or forward payment card numbers, action refunds or cancellations without human sign-off, keep transcripts indefinitely with no stated deletion point, or train a shared model on your customer data by default. Get all four confirmed in writing before launch.
