# Chatbot Companies in the UAE: How to Tell Them Apart

Search for chatbot companies in the UAE and you'll get three kinds of result: agency landing pages that all describe the same service, ranked listicles that are mostly paid placement, and a few genuine specialists buried below both.

The websites won't separate them for you. Every one of them says custom AI, multilingual support, seamless integration. What separates them is a handful of questions that most buyers never ask — and the answers are available if you do.

## The four kinds of company

**Full-service digital agencies.** Chatbots are one line on a menu that also includes websites and social media. Convenient if they already do your marketing. The risk is depth: a bot is a system that needs maintaining, not a campaign that ends.

**Chatbot specialists.** This is the only thing they do. Usually better at the parts that matter — Arabic handling, handover design, integration — and usually smaller, so ask about capacity and what happens if your main contact leaves.

**Platform resellers.** They implement someone else's product. Fine, often cheaper, and the important question becomes what happens if you want to leave the platform.

**Offshore teams with a UAE front.** Very common. Works for a clearly specified build. Strains on local knowledge, timezone support, and Arabic testing with real customers.

None of these is wrong. Knowing which one you're talking to changes what you should ask.

Here is a quick comparison of the four types described above.

| Company type | What it means | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full-service digital agencies | Chatbots are one service among many, like websites and social media | Depth: a bot needs ongoing maintenance, not just a campaign |
| Chatbot specialists | Chatbots are their only focus | Usually smaller, so capacity and staff turnover matter |
| Platform resellers | They implement another company's product | What happens if you want to leave the platform |
| Offshore teams with a UAE front | Common setup, works for a clearly specified build | Local knowledge, timezone support, and Arabic testing |

## The five questions that actually separate them

**1. Can you demo Arabic, live, with my messages?**

The one that sorts the field fastest. Take fifty real messages from your own inbox — Gulf dialect, mixed with English, some typed in Latin characters, typos included — and ask them to run it unprepared.

Almost everyone claims Arabic support. Far fewer handle how UAE customers actually write, and the difference only shows on unrehearsed material.

**2. Whose name is the WhatsApp Business account in?**

If the number your customers message is registered under the vendor's account, your primary customer channel belongs to them. Moving later is painful and sometimes means a new number.

This should be in your company's name. Ask before signing, not after.

**3. Where is conversation data stored?**

Your chat logs are personal data. Which regime applies depends on your licence: mainland companies fall under the federal Personal Data Protection Law, [Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/digital-uae/data/data-protection-laws); DIFC-registered firms fall under DIFC [Data Protection Law No. 5 of 2020](https://www.difc.com/business/laws-and-regulations/legal-database/difc-laws/data-protection-law-difc-law-no-5-2020); ADGM has its own regulations again.

A vendor who says "UAE compliant" without asking where you're licensed hasn't thought about it. Ask for country of storage, whether a UAE region is available, and the retention period.

**4. What happens when it can't answer?**

The handover is the product. Ask what triggers it, whether your agent sees the full conversation or a summary, and what happens outside working hours. A vendor who has only thought about the automation half has built you a wall.

**5. Who maintains the answers after launch?**

Prices change, services change, your bot doesn't — unless someone updates it. Ask whether updates are included, how you request one, and whether you can edit answers yourself. A bot nobody can update is a bot that's wrong within a quarter.

## What the listicles don't tell you

The "top 10 chatbot companies in UAE" posts are directories of who markets hardest. Several read as though the top-ranked firm wrote them. Some are on platforms where placement is bought outright.

Use them for one thing: building a longlist. Then check each company yourself.

**Trade licence.** Ask for it. It shows the legal entity, the licensed activity and the date of incorporation. Mainland firms are licensed by their emirate's economic department — Dubai's DET, Abu Dhabi's [ADDED](https://www.added.gov.ae/en) — and free zone companies by their zone.

**Age of the entity.** A company incorporated eight months ago can be excellent. It just can't have delivered 200 projects.

**Something live.** Ask to see a bot that's running in production right now, with the awkward parts visible, not a case study PDF.

**A reference you pick.** Not the three they offer.

## Pricing, and what gets left out

Quotes in this market are hard to compare because they cover different things. Three costs, and vendors usually quote the first.

**Build or subscription.** The headline number.

**WhatsApp conversation charges.** Billed by Meta, separately, by message category — marketing, utility, authentication and service are priced differently, rates vary by country, and Meta has revised the model more than once. This is not in your vendor's fee.

**Maintenance.** Either their time or yours. Someone has to keep the answers current.

Ask every vendor to quote all three. The cheapest build often has the most expensive year.

The three costs to ask every vendor to quote are summarised below.

| Cost | What it covers | Billed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Build or subscription | The headline number | The vendor |
| WhatsApp conversation charges | Priced by message category: marketing, utility, authentication and service | Meta, separately |
| Maintenance | Keeping the answers current, either their time or yours | Often left out of the quote |

## Signals of a company worth hiring

- **They ask to see your inbox before quoting.** Anyone who quotes without looking at your actual conversations is selling a package.
- **They tell you what not to automate.** Complaints, clinical questions, variable pricing. A vendor who says the bot should handle everything hasn't run one.
- **They raise WhatsApp verification early.** It needs a trade licence and a display-name review, and it's the part of the timeline outside their control. Mentioning it unprompted is a good sign.
- **They talk about who owns what.** Accounts, data, exportability — without being asked.
- **Their Arabic demo is unrehearsed.** And they're comfortable when it stumbles, because they know where the limits are.

## Warning signs

- A fixed price before seeing your conversations.
- "Fully autonomous" as a selling point. Nobody credible promises that for customer-facing work.
- Vague answers on data location.
- No handover design in the proposal.
- The WhatsApp number registered to them.
- Arabic support asserted but never demonstrated on your material.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much do chatbot companies in the UAE charge?**
Widely variable, and the headline build price is the least useful number. Compare total first-year cost: build, platform, WhatsApp conversation charges and maintenance.

**Should I choose a UAE company or an international one?**
Local matters for three things — Arabic testing with real customers, support in your timezone, and integrations specific to this market like WhatsApp Business verification or regional portals. If none of those apply, international is fine.

**How long does it take?**
A focused build is often four to six weeks. WhatsApp verification runs alongside and isn't fully in anyone's control, so start it first.

**What if I want to switch companies later?**
Depends entirely on what you agreed at the start: whose accounts, whether you can export the knowledge base and conversation history, and whether the number is yours. Settle it before signing.

**Do I need a custom build or is a platform enough?**
Platforms are fine for standard flows. Custom earns its cost when you have unusual integrations or when Arabic quality is central to your business.
